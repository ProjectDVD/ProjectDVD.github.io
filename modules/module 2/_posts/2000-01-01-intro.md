### Module 2: Collecting Evidence of Practice
#### Introduction
Now that you’ve thought long and carefully about your professional practice, the value you are interested in highlighting, and the specific aspects of practice aligned with that value, let’s turn our attention to ways you might go about collecting or gathering evidence.

Depending on how you approach things, you might be thinking, “Alright, let’s do this!” and you’ve already started thinking about all of the ways you can collect data that you can use to demonstrate the value of the school library program. Others of you might be thinking, “Seriously, I’ve already got way too many things to do! I don’t have the time or the energy to collect data let alone to analyze it or communicate evidence.” Regardless of where you are on this continuum, you might be best served by first taking a step back and considering the student data that is readily available to you or data you already collect (think: exit tickets after you taught a lesson, student samples of works cited for a research project, graded rubric scores for a project that incorporates the inquiry method, etc.), and then being intentional about how you could collect this data or, if you already collect it, how you can best store and organize it so that it is in a format that allows for easier analysis and interpretation. When data collection, storage, and organization become regular aspects of your practice, EBP is less likely to require a lot of extra time or effort, and you’ll be sure to have the data on hand when you want and need it.
