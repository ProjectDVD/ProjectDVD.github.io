Use the ideas below if you want some creative ways to collect data from and with students.

##### QUANTITATIVE DATA COLLECTION
1. STUDENT VOTING: Collecting input from youth does not need to be high tech. A very simple method for collecting input is to ask them to vote for their preference among several options. They might be shown options on posters and signify their preference with a sticky dot or might drop a token in the jar placed in front of their preference. Double your feedback by coding the tokens. For example, you might assign tokens of different colors based on age. You’ll then be able to see, not only how the total group of youth responded, but to isolate the responses by age to see if younger youth and older youth may have different experiences or feedback.
2. A bullseye, meter, or other scale allows youth to rate the program or level of their experience.
3. Provide a series of cards and ask youth to order the cards based on their feedback. Cards might list various services offered to youth, with the youth asked to order the cards based on how valuable they find that service.

> **Where can I get help with collecting data?**
> - **Your principal:** Your principal likely thinks about student data A LOT and is therefore a great resource if you want students’ standardized test data. He/she probably knows the subjects and topics in which students struggle and the ones in which they excel. Your principal would have a macro view of each grade level’s strengths and areas for improvement.
> - **Teachers:** If you want to know if students are integrating what they learn about the inquiry process or various other information literacy skills into their work, including these skills into the curriculum and assessment rubrics are good ways to do this. When you partner with teachers on their unit and research project plans, take the lead on teaching and assessing information literacy skills that students need to know. Teachers have a better understanding of what specific students struggle with and what areas they need the most help. Collaborating with them to create different solutions for student learning can generate excellent data that reveals student learning.
