### Module 4: Effectively Communicating Evidence of Practice
#### Introduction

> "Evidence-based advocacy allows librarians to eﬀectively communicate the value and relevance of school libraries to their elected representatives, policy makers and external stakeholders" (Koufogiannakis & Brettle, 2016, 139).

The goal of communicating the evidence you’ve collected and analyzed is to share the real, tangible differences the school library program makes to student learning outcomes. Your communication with others should aim to answer the questions:
* How is student learning impacted by the activities and initiatives of the school library?
* What contributions does the school library program make to the overall school curricular program? and
* What difference does the school library make to the success of the school?

On one hand, communicating evidence of your practice is like marketing the library program. How and what you choose to share about the library program and how it impacts learning and teaching in your school could be (and often are) used to make decisions on the value of the library program, in turn influencing future support and funding. On the other hand, sharing evidence of practice is simply to inform your audience of what the library program is **already doing** to support the mission and vision of the school/district and to encourage more use by all members of the school community.

What evidence you share and how you communicate evidence of your practice matters. **[SAY MORE, ADD RESEARCH ABOUT SHARING DATA]**. There are pros and cons to each communication method that you must consider to choose the best method in which to share evidence of practice with your stakeholders. In this module, you will focus on **who** you are communicating with (your audience), **what** you are communicating (your message), and **how** you are communicating your message (your method).
