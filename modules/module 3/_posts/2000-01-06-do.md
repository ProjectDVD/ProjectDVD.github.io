### Module 3: Analyzing Data & Interpeting Findings
#### Do
Once you collect your data, it will be time to analyze the data and interpret the findings. Complete [this worksheet (Google Doc)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18c1vlhwK6h_YkKZWGmVPchZ-p7-q4y_yXRt36t7wOzs/edit?usp=sharing){:target="_blank"} and use appropriate tools mentioned in the "Learn" section of this module to help you analyze your data. 
> **Where can I get help with analyzing and interpreting data?**
> - **Math teachers in your district**: One or more math teachers in your district may have experience with various aspects of data analysis, particularly with quantitative analysis. Reaching out to them may prove helpful when calculating measures of central tendency and measures of variability. Also, you may form new collaborative connections!
> - **Teachers in your district with master’s and doctorates who have taken quantitative or qualitative research methods courses**: Multiple teachers in your school and/or district probably have advanced graduate degrees in programs requiring research methods courses, including quantitative and qualitative research methods. Reach out to them to help with coding and identifying themes. 
> - **Graduate students in your area (if there are colleges/universities nearby)**: If your area has one or more colleges and/or universities, it is possible graduate students might be available for hire for a small fee (or maybe a free dinner) to provide guidance or assist with data analysis.
