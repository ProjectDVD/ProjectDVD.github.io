### Part 1: Create Your Own Golden Circle
*Suggested time: ## minutes*

With the Sinek video and Tucker blog post in mind, spend some time creating your own Golden Circle, identifying your own professional why and purpose for the work you do as a school librarian, how you do what you do, and the activities you do day in and day out. Use [this worksheet (PDF)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1hSO9hLzyGWEHb8mFPWbAzkheSo4lbBxV/view?usp=share_link){:target="_blank"} to help you complete each section of your golden circle.

### Part 2: Connect Your Why to Your School/District Goals & Plans
*Suggested time: ## minutes*

1. **Find and review** your school and/or district documents (i.e., portrait of a graduate profile, improvement plan, etc.).
2. **Identify 1-3 goals** that connect with what the school library program already does or could address.
3. **Brainstorm different types of evidence/data** (direct and indirect) you could use to demonstrate if or how the library is contributing to the goals.
4. (Optional) **Share** your list with one or two trusted colleagues and solicit their feedback.

### Part 3: Complete the "Planning for EBP" Worksheet
*Suggested time: ## minutes*

Use [this worksheet (PDF)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qT7XCYWyF9CnxD9SxsEbr-VsmXEfUdmq/view?usp=share_link){:target="_blank"} to help you create a plan for conducting EBP in the school library. You will work through the following:
1. **Purpose:** What is your purpose for conducting EBP in the school library? Make sure to connect the purpose to the school/district goals/plans.
2. **Questions:** What questions about the school library program and its impact on teaching and learning do you want answered? What do you want to know? Take cues from the [S.M.A.R.T. (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound) Goal format](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/business/smart-goals/){:target="_blank"} and create S.M.A.R.T. questions. Make sure the questions you ask can be answered with data you are able to collect or access and they are connected to the goals/mission of the school/district.

| SMART Question | Not-so-smart question |
| -------------- | --------------------- |
| How does the unit on the inquiry process I teach to 6th graders during their study on Ancient Greece help them find the resources they need to complete their reports? | How does the library program impact student learning? |

3. **Outcomes:** What outcomes do you want to see as a result of the instruction and activities you provide in the library? (e.g. What change(s) do you want to see in student learning in the library?)
4. **Timeline:** Create a timeline for implementing EBP throughout the school year. Include when you will collect data (think about the various events/curricular units/other activities going on throughout the school year and build in the collection of data around these activities), analyze it (don’t wait too long after data collection before you analyze it), and share it with others. List specific dates, times, length of time each activity will take (e.g. 2-3 hours, one week, one month, etc.). You can use the worksheet to create your timeline or create a digital timeline using [Canva](https://www.canva.com/create/infographics/timeline/){:target="_blank"}, [Visme](https://www.visme.co){:target="_blank"}**, or another online software app.

NOTE: Your EBP timeline may change throughout the school year. That is ok! It is not meant to be a static thing you follow without any deviation. Things can, and often do, change. Create your timeline based on the information you know now. You can always revisit and adjust your timeline in the upcoming weeks or months if needed.

> **Where can I get help with EBP?**
> - **The district-level library supervisor** (if your district has one): Your district-level library supervisor is a great person to reach out to for help if you want to incorporate EBP into your practice. They can answer specific questions and address specific concerns you might have about EBP. They can also steer you towards various resources and researchers that are focused on EBP in school libraries.
> - **The school library community** through articles, websites, conferences: If you do not have a district library supervisor, searching the web and getting plugged in to your local/state/national school library associations can be invaluable sources of information and support on your EBP journey.
