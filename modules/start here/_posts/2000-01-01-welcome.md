#### Why is this curriculum important?

In short, unlike [Project Outcome](https://www.ala.org/pla/data/outcomemeasurement) for public and academic librarians, no similar resources or tools specifically designed for the unique context in which school librarians work exist. The field of school librarianship has long identified EBP as a means to displaying and communicating the value of the school library program to the wider school community. While there have been many graduate courses, articles, and conference presentations that focus on EBP for school librarians, no EBP professional development curriculum exists for practicing school librarians that teaches the entire EBP process in an easy-to-follow, sustainable way, allowing busy school librarians with many competing priorities and duties to effectively incorporate EBP into their local everyday work. This curriculum is important because it is the first EBP curriculum specifically created for school librarians that takes into account the multiple competing demands on their time and energy as well as the unique school environments they inhabit.

Much research identifies the value school librarians bring to the schools in which they work. “According to decades of research and dozens of impact studies (Lance & Kachel, 2018), school libraries and the certified librarians who lead them matter. School librarians’ collaborative instructional planning, resource curation, technology leadership, intellectual freedom advocacy, and supportive literacy instruction enhance student learning (Ness et al, 2022) and empower students to think, create, share, and grow in their commitments to inquiry, inclusion, collaboration, curation, exploration, and engagement (American Association of School Librarians (AASL), 2018a). Nationally and internationally, data indicate that one of the most important factors in students’ reading achievement is access to a school library (Krashen, 2021). As importantly, school libraries operate as safe spaces fostering student safety and comfort (Merga, 2020) with school librarians developing relationships with students, building their confidence, and bolstering their social and emotional well-being (Hay, 2005; Merga, 2020).”


#### Why should I go through this curriculum?

This curriculum is for you if you know that what you do as a school librarian is important and indispensable for your school community, yet you don’t know how (or haven’t been able) to communicate that importance to others in a way that resonates with them. “Participation in this PD will result in secondary school librarians being able to collect, analyze, integrate, and share evidence of practice” (from proposal narrative), thus allowing school librarians to understand and communicate their value to others in their school communities using specific evidence that matters to those in their school communities.


#### Who is this curriculum for?

This curriculum is designed with the secondary school librarian in mind. We define a secondary school librarian as anyone working with students in grades 6-12 in a “middle school, junior high school, high school, 6-12 grade-specific campus (e.g. 9th grade center), or a campus housing both middle/junior high and high schools.”


#### What about elementary school librarians?

We didn’t forget you! We recognize the differences between elementary and secondary schools and, therefore, their respective school libraries. As we began thinking about creating an EBP curriculum for school librarians, we decided to focus on secondary school librarians because their situation most closely matched (although not entirely) librarians in public and academic libraries. #### How should I work through this curriculum?

**We recommend working through the modules sequentially from 1 to 4** as the activities in each module build upon each other so that you will have a completed EBP product you can share with others once you finish all the modules.


#### How long will it take to work through the entire curriculum?

The entire curriculum was designed with various professional learning situations in mind. Each module can be completed in <span style="text-decoration:underline;">approximately one hour</span>, but may take longer if you want to dive deeper into a topic and go through all the materials in the “Explore More” section or do further research on your own.


#### Can I use this curriculum to facilitate a professional learning experience for all the librarians in my school district?

Yes! Absolutely! (Do we want to create a leader/facilitator guide?)


#### Are there any additional materials I need to work through the curriculum?

You will need a computer, laptop, or tablet with internet access to work through all the modules. Depending on the module, there are worksheets that you can print and use to complete the various activities. PDFs of individual worksheets are linked throughout each module, but all the worksheets can be downloaded as a PDF packet here. A pen or pencil is also needed to complete the written activities and reflections in each module.
